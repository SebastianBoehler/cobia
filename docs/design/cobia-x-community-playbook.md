# Cobia X and community playbook

Status: canonical living guide

Updated: 2026-08-21

This playbook turns the Cobia brand into repeatable publishing behavior. It
borrows the useful part of Jumper's strategy: polished, recognizable product
posts on the main timeline and a warmer, funnier voice in replies. It does not
copy Jumper's creative, color, phrasing, or meme style.

## Channel job

The main timeline builds trust and product understanding. Replies build
familiarity and community. Reposts add context only when Cobia has something
specific to contribute.

| Surface | Job | Default voice |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Main posts | Explain one product truth and show proof | Polished and concise |
| Threads | Teach one mechanism or unpack evidence | Clear and technical |
| Replies | Participate, help, joke, and learn | Human and context-aware |
| Quote posts | Add a Cobia-specific angle | Sharp and relevant |
| Incident posts | State impact, boundary, and next update | Serious and factual |

## Profile

### Recommended bio

> AI proposes. Cobia verifies. Your wallet approves exact calls. An intent
> exchange and transaction firewall built on X Layer.

Short alternative:

> State the outcome. Keep the keys. Verified intents on X Layer.

The bio should describe the current verifier boundary, not only DeFi route
comparison. Use `getcobia.com` as the single profile link.

### Pinned post

Pin one evergreen product proof, not a generic launch announcement. It should:

1. State the user's outcome.
2. Show the signed limits or policy.
3. Show proposal, verification, and wallet approval as separate steps.
4. Link one confirmed X Layer transaction or receipt.
5. End with one invitation: try an intent or build a solver.

Refresh the pin when a materially stronger proof replaces it. Do not pin every
new feature.

## Content pillars

### 1. Confirmed outcomes

Lead with what happened, then explain the boundary.

> First Cobia intent, confirmed on X Layer.
>
> 1 USDt0 -> 0.999471 USDG.
>
> Cobia verified the program. The wallet approved the exact calls.
>
> Inspect the transaction: [link]

Use exact values and evidence. Never manufacture a larger or cleaner result.

### 2. Product moments

Show one interaction in 6-15 seconds: an entity resolves, a policy field becomes
explicit, a proposal is rejected, or a receipt confirms. One clip, one lesson.

> Three mentions. Three explicit inputs.
>
> Cobia resolves assets and networks before solvers compete.

Do not publish several near-identical clips in the same day. Prefer one strong
asset and use replies to extend the conversation.

### 3. Verification contrasts

Make the category difference memorable without attacking competitors.

> An agent can search broadly.
>
> It still cannot sign.
>
> Cobia verifies the exact program before your wallet sees it.

Useful contrasts: prompt versus policy, proposal versus authorization, fork
replay versus production, estimate versus confirmed outcome.

### 4. X Layer ecosystem

Show how Cobia makes X Layer more useful: attributable activity, safer agent
demand, new solver supply, merchant services, and protocol integrations.

Tag ecosystem accounts only when the post is directly relevant. A tag is not a
distribution strategy.

### 5. Builder participation

Invite concrete contributions: build a solver, inspect a verifier module, add a
merchant manifest, reproduce a transaction, or challenge a policy assumption.

> The solver can be creative. The verifier cannot be vague.
>
> Here is the capability contract: [link]

## Publishing grammar

### Product post

Use three beats:

1. **Claim:** one sentence that stands alone.
2. **Mechanism:** one or two sentences explaining why it is true.
3. **Proof or action:** one link and one next step.

Example:

> A prompt is not permission.
>
> Cobia turns an outcome into explicit limits and independently verifies the
> exact calls before wallet review.
>
> Try an intent: getcobia.com

### Demo post

Use: `moment -> meaning -> invitation`.

> Watch @USDG, @USDt0, and @XLayer become typed policy inputs.
>
> Solvers compete against explicit limits, not an ambiguous prompt.
>
> Try it: getcobia.com

### Proof post

Use: `outcome -> authority -> evidence`.

> Swap confirmed on X Layer.
>
> The owner wallet approved only the verified calls.
>
> Transaction: [link]

### Technical thread

Use no more than five posts:

1. Problem in plain language.
2. The signed boundary.
3. What the solver may do.
4. What the verifier reproduces.
5. Evidence and builder invitation.

Do not start with an architecture diagram or repository statistics.

## Reply voice

Replies may be quicker, warmer, and funnier than main posts. They still need to
sound like Cobia: observant, precise, and calm.

### Good reply behaviors

- Answer real questions before promoting the product.
- Reference the exact post, protocol, or joke in front of you.
- Use one-line reactions when the conversation does not need a pitch.
- Share a meme only when the connection is obvious without explanation.
- Admit limits directly: `Not supported yet` beats speculative enthusiasm.
- Move technical detail into a thread or docs link when it helps the reader.

### Reply examples

Product launch:

> big fan of agents doing the homework and wallets keeping the final say

Unsafe automation joke:

> autonomous until the calldata asks for adult supervision

X Layer builder shipping a new integration:

> one more useful thing an intent can resolve to. congrats on shipping.

Question about whether Cobia holds keys:

> It does not. Solvers propose unsigned programs; your wallet approves the exact
> calls only after independent verification.

### Avoid

- Forced memes under serious security, loss, or incident posts.
- Generic replies such as `Huge`, `LFG`, `Bullish`, or `This is the future`.
- Turning every conversation into a Cobia pitch.
- Mocking users for risk questions or failed transactions.
- Implying endorsement, partnership, or integration from a casual interaction.
- Using engagement bots or repetitive reply templates.

## Visual publishing system

Use the brand families from `docs/design/cobia-brand-system.md`.

| Family | Best use |
| --- | --- |
| Outcome thread | Short product explainers and launch clips |
| Policy receipt | Limits, before/after states, and educational posts |
| Verifier signal | Security boundary and architecture posts |
| Evidence row | Transactions, deployments, Builder Code, and receipts |

Rules:

- One audience-facing claim per asset.
- Product capture is preferred to abstract illustration.
- Use a 16:9 master for demos; cut platform variants from the same source.
- Keep captions readable without audio and preserve the action on mobile crops.
- Use the same logo position, type scale, cobalt role, and closing frame.
- A teaser may frame evidence; it may not fabricate evidence.

## Cadence

Use a sustainable weekly rhythm after the hackathon:

- 1 confirmed outcome or evidence post.
- 1 product moment or short demo.
- 1 mechanism, builder, or ecosystem post.
- Meaningful replies on 3-5 relevant conversations on active days.

Do not fill a quota with repetitive posts. A real proof is worth more than a
week of generic claims.

## Thirty-day sequence

### Week 1: establish the category

- Pin the strongest end-to-end proof.
- Update the bio to the verifier boundary.
- Publish `prompt is not permission` with a product capture.
- Reply to X Layer builders with specific, non-promotional observations.

### Week 2: make the mechanism legible

- Show a proposal becoming an explicit policy.
- Show one unsafe or unsupported proposal being rejected.
- Publish a short verifier thread with reproduction evidence.

### Week 3: grow supply

- Invite an external solver and document the smallest integration path.
- Feature one protocol or merchant only after its capability is real.
- Ask builders one narrow question that can influence the product.

### Week 4: show compounding value

- Compare two confirmed outcomes or solver behaviors without vanity metrics.
- Publish what Cobia learned and what remains unsupported.
- Replace the pinned post only if the new evidence is materially stronger.

## Measurement

Track signals that correspond to the product:

1. Qualified visits to the intent and solver paths.
2. Intent starts and policy reviews.
3. Confirmed outcomes and inspectable receipts.
4. External solver, merchant, or protocol conversations.
5. Replies that become product questions, reproductions, or contributions.

Likes and follower count are context, not the objective. Never trade truthful
claims for reach.

## Preflight checklist

Before publishing:

- Is there exactly one main claim?
- Is the claim live, estimated, rejected, or planned—and labelled correctly?
- Does the copy separate the agent, verifier, and wallet?
- Can the proof link be opened by someone outside the team?
- Does the asset remain clear without audio?
- Is the CTA specific and proportionate?
- Would the reply still make sense if the Cobia name were removed?
- Does a joke avoid minimizing financial or security risk?
